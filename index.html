CVG Lab, ETH Zurich

3D Conditioning of Diffusion Models for Human Video Generation

Master's Thesis Research Work, Supervised by Prof. Marc Pollefeys, Dr. Manuel Kaufmann, and Dr. Hsuan-I Ho

Report Presentation (PPTX)

Classical physics-based simulation is tedious and slow in practice, and while video diffusion models generate convincing people, they have no real grasp, and thus respect, of 3D structure. This challenge sits at the root of advancements in the domain of 3D, especially for human-centric applications such as animation and virtual try-ons.

I tested several 3D representations (meshes, single- and multi-view normal maps) and approaches to inject 3D information into Wan2.2 and HunyuanVideo-1.5 image-to-video diffusion models. These included ControlNet trainings, existing attention augmentation, and cross-attention injections to name a few. The final approach was a trainable lightweight encoder that injected 3D features into the core Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with new attention heads at spaced intervals.

For Wan2.2, I used the Wan-Animate for motion transfer to allow comparison to ground truth. I worked with the 4D-Dress dataset, and evaluated with pose alignment (2D keypoints and 3D SMPL-X), SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS, FVD and VBench. For HunyuanVideo-1.5, I used a similar evaluation setup but quantitatively limited to LPIPS and VBench as text-guided generation does not align with ground-truth motion.

My results successfully demonstrated that the injected 3D information was used in generated videos, even with a lightweight encoder and minimal injections, but treated it as rigid rather than deformable. The work showed promising results, and clearly pointed to the next step in representing the deformability of 3D features in diffusion models.

Investigating Relational Reasoning in Vision-Language Models (VLMs)

Research Project, Supervised by Yifan Hou

Code Report

Do Vision-Language Models (VLMs) truly understand relational information and reason about it for objects seen in images, or do they mimic logical reasoning?

To test this hypothesis, we first built a simple custom 2D shapes dataset that made objects of basic shapes and colors located in various configurations in relation to each other. Furthermore, masked variants were created to remove visual information, and prompts were carefully designed to test how the model responds to given information (present or missing). We used the Qwen-VL model with our dataset to analyze outputs quantitatively, and also used layer-wise linear probing during inference to trace where visual and relational information is processed.

We identified strong patterns in how language and visual information are handled across the architecture. We showed that while the model processes information as expected for visual-identification tasks, it fails to fire reasoning-related activity for visual-relational tasks. Thus, we established that current VLM models use shortcut strategies that mimic logical reasoning.

AIT Lab, ETHZ

Reconstructing Photorealistic Garments from Videos

Practical Research Work, Supervised by Artur Grigorev & Yufeng Zheng

Code Presentation (Keynote)

The Gaussian Garments project used 4D-Dress, a multi-view capture dataset of clothed humans in diverse motions. However, other such datasets, such as ActorsHQ vary in terms of viewing angles, camera counts, and subject distances. This proved challenging for the COLMAP-based mesh reconstruction stage, the first step of the pipeline, as it remained brittle to variations in the setup.

I adapted the mesh reconstruction stage with a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) based approach. This removed reliance on Poisson-based dense reconstruction from COLMAP, and thus made the entire pipeline robust to new datasets.

Since 3DGS produces a gaussians point-cloud, I customized the GS2Mesh pipeline to improve mesh extraction and 3D mesh segmentation.

The result was a generalized and robust pipeline for mesh reconstruction from static multi-view frames, which allowed generation of segmented garments using simple prompts such as "beige pants" or "blue shirt". The project is open-sourced on GitHub.

3D Vision Course, ETHZ

Metric 3D Reconstruction from Diffusion-Based Monocular Depth Prediction

Research Project, Supervised by Dr. Zuria Bauer & Mihai Dusmanu

Report Poster

Can 3D structure be recovered at real-world scale from ordinary 2D RGB images without camera extrinsics?

We developed an end-to-end pipeline around the Marigold depth estimator for metric depth estimation and 3D scene reconstruction. We first converted the output from Marigold into metric depth using simple L1 loss against ground-truth depth maps. Then, using the transformed metric depth estimates, we extracted features using SIFT, and used them to align point clouds with camera intrinsics. The alignment was then passed into RANSAC for initial refinement, and then into ICP for final optimization.

We ran the full pipeline and demonstrated its effectiveness and robustness on the KITTI outdoor dataset.

Digital Humans Course, ETHZ

Fast Clothed Human Avatar Synthesis

Research Project, Supervised by Zhiyin Qian

Report Presentation (PPTX)

Clothed human avatars from the ARAH pipeline render beautifully but slowly due to dependence on expensive per-frame marching-cubes-based surface extraction.

We extended the Fast-ARAH pipeline to bypass marching cubes by introducing a pose-dependent MLP to model non-rigid skeleton deformations. This made the marching cubes only necessary for the first iteration, rather than every iteration, for mesh extraction.

Our approach resulted in about 100x faster rendering after the first inference step, with only a slight drop in render quality.

  • Neural Rendering
  • SDF
  • Marching Cubes

Foundations of Reinforcement Learning Course, ETHZ

Large-Scale MARL for Traffic Signal Optimization

Research Project, Supervised by Batuhan Yardim

Report Poster

Can road networks spanning thousands of traffic lights be modelled and controlled within a single intelligent system?

Traffic signals can not be managed as stand-alone agents, since intersections serve as nodes in a larger network, and traffic at one intersection affects the flow at others. In addition, traffic flow dynamically varies throughout the day due to predictable and unpredictable factors.

To tackle this challenge, we novelly modeled signal control as a multi-agent environment with inter-agent communication as the primary sequence problem. The spatial dependencies between agents were treated as a 2D sequence and processed by a Transformer model. In addition, we built a synthetic data generator to produce varying complexity road networks along with complex and dynamic traffic flows to bypass the scarcity of limited real-world data insufficient to train transformers.

Our framework achieved competitive performance with limited state information. The final system was a scalable multi-agent RL framework, enabling global agent communication and adapting to road networks of varying sizes without retraining.

  • Synthetic Data Generation
